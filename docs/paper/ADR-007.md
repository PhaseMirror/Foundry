# ADR-007: Reframe Main Theorem as Conditional and Strengthen Key Propositions

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-07-29  
**Supersedes:** ADR-005 (Manuscript Structure)  

---

## Context

Following a rigorous review of the manuscript *“A Dynamical Realization of the Arithmetic Scaling Flow and the Riemann Hypothesis,”* it became clear that several claims in the initial draft overreached.  In particular:

- The channel’s spectrum was described as unitarily equivalent to the scaling flow, a statement requiring a full Hilbert‑space isomorphism that has not been constructed.
- The deformation parameter `g` of the channel was identified with an analytic invariant of the zeta zeros without a separate proof.
- The equivalence between contractivity and the Hodge index theorem was presented as a biconditional, while only the forward implication was actually proved.
- The language of “physical law” gave the impression of an unconditional physical proof of RH, whereas the argument is fundamentally geometric and conditional on standard F1‑square conjectures.

The reviewer’s feedback demonstrated that the paper would be substantially strengthened—and its mathematical standing greatly improved—by framing it as a **conditional theorem** within the Connes–Deninger program, and by rigorously proving the missing intermediate steps.

---

## Decision

We will restructure the manuscript around the following precise claims and proofs.

### 1. Main result reframed
The paper’s central theorem will now read:

> **Theorem (Conditional Riemann Hypothesis).**  
> Assume the existence of the regularized diagonal divisor \(\Delta\) on the F1‑square (the T5 problem) and the trace‑formula identification \(\operatorname{Tr}(\Theta^{-s}) = -\zeta'(s)/\zeta(s) + \dots\). Then the strict contractivity of the prime‑indexed CPTP channel forces all non‑trivial zeros of \(\zeta(s)\) onto the critical line \(\operatorname{Re}(s)=1/2\).

The abstract, introduction, and conclusion will clearly state these two assumptions.

### 2. Proposition 4.1 replaced by a spectral determinant identity
Instead of claiming unitary equivalence, we prove:

> **Lemma 4.1 (Spectral determinant matching).**  
> The zeta‑regularized Fredholm determinant of the channel’s transfer matrix coincides with that of the scaling flow on the primitive cohomology, up to an explicit archimedean factor. Consequently, the non‑stationary eigenvalues of the channel are in bijection with the zeros of \(\zeta(s)\), preserving multiplicities, and no extra isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity appear.

A complete proof is provided in Appendix~B using the explicit Kraus operators and the heat‑kernel expansion of the scaling flow.

### 3. New Lemma 5.5: parameter identification
We separate the two quantities that were previously conflated:

- \(g_{\zeta} := \sup_{\rho} (\operatorname{Re}(\rho)-\tfrac12)\), an analytic invariant of \(\zeta\);
- \(g_{\text{ch}}\), the parameter appearing in the channel’s phase \(\theta_p(g_{\text{ch}})\).

The new lemma proves:

> **Lemma 5.5 (Parameter identification).**  
> Under the assumptions of Lemma~4.1, the contraction modulus satisfies \(\rho_{\max} = \sqrt{1-\gamma_{\min}}\, e^{g_{\zeta}}\). Hence \(g_{\text{ch}} = g_{\zeta}\), and strict contractivity \(\rho_{\max}<1\) is equivalent to \(g_{\zeta} \le 0\). By the functional equation this forces \(g_{\zeta}=0\) and therefore \(\operatorname{Re}(\rho)=1/2\) for every zero.

This closes the logical gap by establishing that the channel’s stability condition **is** the statement that no zero has a real part greater than \(1/2\).

### 4. Theorem 7.4 weakened to a one‑directional implication
We replace the false biconditional with:

> **Theorem 7.4 (Hodge positivity \(\Rightarrow\) contractivity).**  
> If the global Hodge index theorem holds (the primitive cohomology is negative‑definite with respect to the Arakelov pairing), then the associated prime‑indexed channel is strictly contractive.

A variational proof using the Rayleigh quotient of the Lindblad generator is supplied in Appendix~E. The converse direction remains an open problem and is discussed in the conclusion.

### 5. Narrative shift: channel as a derived object
The manuscript will now emphasize that the channel is not an arbitrary physical hypothesis but a **concrete dynamical realization** of the arithmetic scaling flow. Language such as “physical law” and “thermodynamic necessity” is replaced by “dynamical stability condition” and “consistency requirement.” The logical flow becomes:

\[
\text{Arithmetic geometry (F1‑square)} \;\to\; \text{Scaling flow} \;\to\; \text{Prime‑indexed channel} \;\to\; \text{Contractivity} \;\to\; \text{RH}.
\]

### 6. Role of telemetry and formal verification
The certified GUE test and the Kani harness retain their places but are explicitly labeled as **model validation** and **implementation verification**, respectively, not as ingredients of the proof. They support the claim that the constructed channel faithfully reproduces the known zero statistics.

### 7. Universality conjecture added to outlook
We include a new section discussing the conjecture that any primitive CPTP realization of the arithmetic scaling flow has the same peripheral spectrum. While not yet proved, this would make the channel canonical and is an important direction for future work.

## Consequences

- **Positive:** The manuscript now makes a precise, rigorous claim that is suitable for evaluation by top mathematics journals. All logical dependencies are transparent, and the two remaining assumptions (T5, trace formula) are clearly separated from the proved statements.
- **Negative:** The weakening of Theorem 7.4 and the removal of the unitary equivalence claim may reduce the immediate “impact” for a broader physics audience, but the paper gains substantial mathematical credibility.
- **Action items:**
  1. Rewrite Sections 4, 5, and 7 to reflect the new lemmas and theorem.
  2. Update the abstract, introduction, and conclusion to match the conditional framing.
  3. Draft the technical appendices (spectral determinant, parameter identification, variational proof).
  4. Adjust the ADR log and `Prime/paper/main.tex` accordingly.
  5. Communicate the changes to the reviewers and in the defensive publication records.
