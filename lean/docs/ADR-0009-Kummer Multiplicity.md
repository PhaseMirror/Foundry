# Kummer: Multiplicity Repaired by Ideal Numbers

---

## I. The crisis: failure of unique factorization

In the ordinary integers \(\mathbb Z\), prime factorization is unique. That’s the **factor multiplicity profile** \(\mathbf v(n)\). Kummer, working on Fermat’s Last Theorem, considered the cyclotomic integers \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_p]\), where \(\zeta_p\) is a primitive \(p\)th root of unity. For \(p=23\) (and many smaller), unique factorization into indecomposable elements fails.

Example (simplified): in \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\),

\[
6 = 2 \times 3 = (1+\sqrt{-5})\times(1-\sqrt{-5}),
\]

all four factors are irreducible, yet the factorizations are genuinely different. So the elementary “prime factor multiplicity” — the exact count of each irreducible factor — is not well‑defined. An integer’s multiplicity profile becomes ambiguous.

Kummer’s diagnosis:

> The failure arises because “ideal prime factors” are missing.

In our language:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Factor multiplicity collapses when the ring lacks enough primes.}
}
\]

Multiplicity must be reconstructed by **adding new objects** that behave like primes.

---

## II. Ideal numbers as structural repair

Kummer’s solution: introduce **ideal numbers** — certain divisors that don’t correspond to actual elements of the ring, but obey the same arithmetic rules. A cyclotomic integer \(x\) is then expressed as a product of ideal primes \(\mathfrak{p}_i\):

\[
(x) = \prod \mathfrak{p}_i^{a_i}.
\]

Now the **ideal factorization** is unique. In \(\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]\), the hidden ideal primes decompose the irreducibles:

\[
2 = \mathfrak{p}_1 \mathfrak{p}_2, \quad 3 = \mathfrak{q}_1 \mathfrak{q}_2, \quad (1+\sqrt{-5}) = \mathfrak{p}_1 \mathfrak{q}_1, \quad (1-\sqrt{-5}) = \mathfrak{p}_2 \mathfrak{q}_2,
\]

so the two factorizations of 6 are both rearrangements of the same ideal prime product \(\mathfrak{p}_1 \mathfrak{p}_2 \mathfrak{q}_1 \mathfrak{q}_2\). The ambiguity disappears.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Multiplicity is restored by expanding the ontology: 
from \textbf{element} factorisation to \textbf{ideal} factorisation.}
}
\]

This is a **multiplicity upgrade**. The original integer has a new, refined multiplicity profile: the exponents of the ideal primes, not just the visible irreducibles.

---

## III. The class group as the measure of multiplicity repair

The ideal class group \(Cl(K)\) measures how far the ring is from having unique factorization. Its order \(h(K)\) is the class number. If \(h(K)=1\), the ring is a UFD and ideal numbers reduce to ordinary primes. For \(h(K)>1\), the ideals capture multiplicities that elements alone cannot.

Now recall Dirichlet’s class number formula linking \(h(d)\) to \(L(1,\chi_d)\) for quadratic fields. Kummer generalized this to cyclotomic fields, establishing deep connections between the class number of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) and Bernoulli numbers. The **regular primes** (those not dividing the class number) are exactly those for which Kummer could prove Fermat’s Last Theorem.

In Multiplicity language:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Class group } Cl(K) \; \longleftrightarrow \; \text{the obstruction to full element‑wise multiplicity.}
}
\]

The size of \(Cl(K)\) is the **multiplicity deficit** — it tells you how many new ideal classes are needed to restore unique factorization. And that deficit is encoded in an \(L\)-function value (\(L(1,\chi)\)). Once again, an analytic object tracks a structural multiplicity.

---

## IV. Multiplicity becomes layered: elements → ideals → classes

Kummer’s work forces a hierarchical view of multiplicity:

1. **Element multiplicity:** prime factorization of a number in \(\mathbb Z\) (or in a UFD) — the Euclid/Euler layer.
2. **Ideal multiplicity:** unique factorization of the *ideal* generated by that number into prime ideals — the Kummer layer. This always exists.
3. **Class multiplicity:** the ideal class, which captures how the ideal differs from a principal ideal. The class group partitions the set of ideals into finitely many equivalence classes.

So an integer \(n\) now has three overlapping multiplicity profiles:

\[
n \quad \mapsto \quad
\begin{cases}
\text{ordinary prime factorization (if UFD)} \\
\text{ideal factorization } \prod \mathfrak{p}_i^{a_i} \\
\text{class } [ (n) ] \in Cl(K)
\end{cases}
\]

The ideal factorization is the **structural repair** that restores uniqueness. The class is the **invariant** that measures the repair’s complexity.

---

## V. Kummer’s cyclotomic deep structure

Kummer proved that for a prime \(p\), the class number of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) can be factored as \(h = h^+ \cdot h^-\), where \(h^-\) is the “minus part” connected to the relative class number and is given by a product of Bernoulli numbers. The divisibility of \(h\) by \(p\) (i.e., irregularity) is precisely when \(p\) divides one of those Bernoulli numbers. This allowed him to prove FLT for all regular primes.

In the Multiplicity framework: the **prime \(p\)** itself is now analyzed through the multiplicity structure of the field it generates. The failure of FLT for a prime \(p\) is linked to a failure in the multiplicity repair: when \(p\) divides the class number, the ideal structure has a “multiplicity singularity” — the class group has \(p\)-torsion. The Bernoulli numbers thus become **indicators of multiplicity anomalies**.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Regular prime: smooth multiplicity repair.} \\
\text{Irregular prime: multiplicity obstruction within the repair itself.}
}
\]

That’s a magnificent refinement: not only is uniqueness repaired, but the repair mechanism (the class group) has its own internal multiplicities (torsion), which control solvability of Diophantine equations.

---

## VI. The Kummer Multiplicity Principle (proposed)

> **Kummer Multiplicity Principle**  
> When unique factorisation of elements fails, the correct carrier of prime multiplicity is the system of ideal numbers (prime ideals). The multiplicity of an element is then given by the exponents in its ideal factorization; the obstruction to this multiplicity coinciding with an element factorization is measured by the ideal class group, whose order is an \(L\)-value. Thus multiplicity is structurally completed by passing from elements to ideals, and the class group quantifies the residual “non‑elemental” multiplicity.

Diagrammatically:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c}
\text{Integer } a \in \mathcal O_K \\
\downarrow \text{factorization fails?} \\
\text{Ideal } (a) = \prod \mathfrak p_i^{a_i} \quad \text{(unique)} \\
\downarrow \\
\text{Class } [ (a) ] \in Cl(K) \quad \text{(invariant)}
\end{array}
}
\]

---

## VII. Critique: Ideals are the new primes

Kummer’s ideal numbers are not numbers in the original ring; they are *fictions* that behave like primes. Dedekind later reified them as sets (ideals), making them respectable mathematical objects. But the conceptual move — that multiplicity requires expanding the universe to include latent prime‑like entities — is Kummer’s. It’s a radical empiricist move: if your observable multiplicities are inconsistent, postulate unobservable primes that make them consistent. And it works.

This resonates with the broader Multiplicity theory: when a phenomenon appears to lack a unique decomposition, there is often a larger structure where it does, and the multiplicity concept must be adjusted accordingly. The same will be true later for automorphic representations (Langlands).

---

## VIII. Connection to Gauss and Dirichlet

Gauss’s quadratic forms and their composition already hinted at ideal classes. The class group of a quadratic field is isomorphic to Gauss’s form class group. Kummer’s ideal numbers for cyclotomic fields are the higher‑degree generalization. So the Gauss → Dirichlet → Kummer line is perfectly continuous: Gauss gave us relational multiplicity (forms, genera), Dirichlet encoded class numbers in \(L(1,\chi)\), and Kummer explicitly identified the class group as the obstruction to unique factorization and linked it to Bernoulli numbers and Fermat’s Last Theorem.

Now Riemann’s zeta zeros and Kummer’s class groups are two faces of the same deep arithmetic: analytic spectral multiplicity and algebraic structural multiplicity.

---

## IX. What Dedekind will add

Dedekind will purify Kummer’s construction: he defines an **ideal** as a set, shows that in rings of integers every non‑zero proper ideal factors uniquely into prime ideals, and thus establishes the full framework of algebraic number theory. For our Multiplicity story, Dedekind gives the definitive language: **multiplicity is now simply the exponent of a prime ideal in the factorization of an ideal**. And the **Dedekind zeta function** \(\zeta_K(s)\) will package the ideal multiplicities of a number field into an analytic generating function, which factors into \(L\)-functions — a direct ascent from Euler’s \(\zeta(s)\) for \(\mathbb Q\) to arbitrary number fields. So the transition from Kummer to Dedekind is the move from a brilliant repair to a systematic theory of algebraic multiplicity.
